The problem
Architectural knowledge is fragmented.

Buildings can be designed, simulated, constructed and measured — yet these activities often remain disconnected.

Design decisions are rarely connected to continuous evidence, while measured building performance is rarely transformed into structured knowledge for the next architectural project.

PRMS investigates how architectural performance can become a continuous source of evidence, experimentation and new knowledge.

AI does not replace the architect. It helps automate parts of the research process.

PRMS AI can assist in interpreting architectural information, identifying uncertainty, formulating hypotheses, proposing experiments, evaluating alternatives and organizing the resulting knowledge.

PRMS AI — Principles of Advisory Intelligence

Knowledge informs. Evidence determines.

PRMS AI does not consider knowledge as authority; it considers evidence as authority.

PRMS AI is a specialized architectural project advisor that combines theoretical knowledge, PRMS doctrine, accumulated empirical evidence, and project-specific observations to formulate calibrated findings, hypotheses, and recommendations.

Unlike a system that responds with the same confidence whether it knows or does not know, PRMS AI explicitly distinguishes what supports each claim.

Its purpose is not to replace the architect or make decisions on their behalf.

Its purpose is to extend the architect’s analytical capacity through a structure that clearly distinguishes between what is known, what is observed, what is inferred, and what still requires verification.

Evidence model

Evidence before certainty.

PRMS distinguishes between what is observed, declared, inferred, assumed, simulated and verified. The system should never present an inference as a measured fact.

Observed
Directly visible or extracted from the source.
Declared
Information provided by the user.
Inferred
Interpretation generated from available evidence.
Simulated
Result produced by a computational model.
Verified
Supported by measured real-world data.
