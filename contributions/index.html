Summary

Shahid proposed a five-stage conceptual structure for architectural intelligence:

Observe → Understand → Explain → Learn → Act & Evaluate

His contribution emphasizes continuous feedback, validation, and the distinction between correlation and causation in the development of reliable architectural intelligence.

Relevance to PRMS

This contribution is relevant to the ongoing development of the PRMS Architectural Intelligence research direction, particularly regarding continuous learning, performance feedback, and evidence-based regulation.

Madhumita Roy

Jadavpur University — Department of Architecture

ResearchGate · 2026
Contribution

Passive Survivability and the Limits of Conventional Sustainability Assessment

Summary

Roy highlighted a critical gap in conventional sustainability assessment: building performance cannot be understood independently from occupant functions and operational conditions.

Her contribution argues that a building may perform efficiently under normal operating conditions while remaining unable to maintain habitable conditions when occupant functions or supporting conditions are disrupted.

Relevance to PRMS

This contribution is relevant to the development of PRMS, particularly its distinction between efficiency, autonomy, resilience, and habitability. It reinforces the need to evaluate architectural performance not only under normal operating conditions, but also in relation to dependency, continuity, and the capacity of the built environment to maintain habitable conditions under changing or disrupted conditions.

Craig Carlton Dremann

Craig Carlton Dremann Computer Ecosystems™

ResearchGate · 2026
Contribution

Ecological Memory and Cumulative Architectural Intelligence

Summary

Dremann introduced an ecological perspective on architectural intelligence, comparing the learning capacity of intelligent systems with the long-term memory and adaptive behavior of natural ecosystems.

His contribution emphasizes that current AI design systems often operate as isolated tools, generating or optimizing individual projects without retaining the real-world lessons produced by previous buildings.

In contrast, natural ecosystems continuously accumulate environmental evidence over time. Rainfall, heat, wind, soil conditions, resource flows, and seasonal changes become part of an evolving system memory that influences future behavior.

Observe → Accumulate → Remember → Adapt → Improve

Dremann therefore suggests that a true architectural intelligence would require persistent memory capable of retaining real-world performance evidence across buildings, climates, materials, and users.

Relevance to PRMS

This contribution is particularly relevant to the development of the PRMS Architectural Intelligence research direction, especially the concept of cumulative architectural knowledge.

The ecological analogy of a landscape retaining the memory of previous environmental conditions provides a conceptual basis for treating buildings not only as objects of design, but also as sources of continuous performance evidence.

This perspective connects directly with the emerging Discovery Knowledge Core, which is intended to accumulate evidence from projects, simulations, measured performance, and external knowledge in order to discover patterns across buildings, environmental conditions, and operational contexts.

In this context, architectural intelligence is not simply a system with more memory. It becomes an infrastructure through which architecture can develop cumulative memory and evidence-based learning.

Cara Tonucci

Somatic Rising Institute — Structural Stability Science

ResearchGate · 2026
Contribution

Architectural Intelligence and Underlying Performance Conditions

Summary

Tonucci raised an important structural question about how intelligent systems interpret performance within complex environments.

A measurable performance condition does not necessarily provide a complete explanation of why that condition occurs. Energy use, maintenance demand, occupancy, resource consumption, operational instability, and other observable variables may reflect conditions that have accumulated or remained unresolved elsewhere in the system.

Her contribution therefore challenges architectural intelligence to look beyond isolated observations and consider the underlying conditions that may contribute to observed performance.

Observe → Characterize → Relate → Explain → Learn

This perspective suggests that reliable architectural intelligence requires more than identifying patterns: it must progressively investigate the relationships between observed outcomes and the conditions that produce them.

Relevance to PRMS

This contribution is relevant to the development of the PRMS Diagnostic and the broader PRMS Architectural Intelligence research direction.

PRMS seeks to move from isolated performance measurements toward an integrated understanding of how architectural, environmental, operational, and human conditions interact.

The contribution reinforces the need for a diagnostic system to distinguish between an observed performance condition and the underlying relationships that may be contributing to it.

This perspective also informs the emerging Discovery Knowledge Core, where evidence from projects, simulations, measured performance, and external research can be accumulated to identify relationships and formulate new hypotheses.

The resulting research question is: Can an architectural intelligence learn to distinguish what a building is doing from the conditions that are contributing to that behavior?
